The BRAM prediction was wrong, and wrong in the direction that helps
the event-driven design: doubling K from 4 to 8 cost **one** tile and
~108 logic LUTs, not a doubling. Banking splits the same bits across
more, shallower banks; each bank still rounds up to whole RAMB18s, and
at these sizes the rounding is what sets the count. (The "2x" guess
treated banks as additive instead of a partition of the same memory —
a reasoning error, now on record.) Two thirds of the fabric is the DMA
and interconnect (2,700 LUTs); the engine itself is 1,262 LUTs.

## What this settles and what it does not

- The K axis of the cycle model, 45.2k + 90.3k/K, has two silicon
  points (K=4, K=8), both within 2 % after the constant offset; the
  K-independent floor is real on hardware.
- The matched-parallelism verdict at K = P = 8 still needs Build 4
  (dense P=8, expected ~543 us flat). ED K=8 at 575.5 us would then
  lose by ~6 % at the mean and still win on its two fastest samples —
  the prediction on record.
- K=8 is nearly free in BRAM. The energy cost of 8 banks switching per
  spike is the open question (C0025, gated on the meter).
