# Literature review

One row per paper in `~/OneDrive - Carleton University/Research Papers/Similar Papers/`.
Every row was produced by reading the paper, not the abstract. Numbers are the
paper's own; where a paper does not state something the cell says so rather than
estimating. "Future Works" is taken from the paper's own future-work or
conclusion section, and reads NOT STATED when the paper offers none.

**Tags.** `robot`, `snn`, `fpga`, applied only where they genuinely fit. Papers
fitting none carry `other` with a short reason; forcing a tag would make the
column useless. Loihi is neuromorphic silicon, not reconfigurable logic, so
Loihi papers take `snn` and not `fpga`.

Assembled 2026-09-20. See the notes below the table for duplicates, scope
flags, and the energy-provenance summary that matters most to this thesis.

## Notes on the corpus

**Duplicates (34 unique papers in 36 files).**
- `Firefly-P_FPGA-Accelerated_Spiking_Neural_Network.pdf` and `2601.21222v1.pdf` are byte-identical.
- `Fully_neuromorphic_drone.pdf` and `fully_neuro_drone.pdf` are byte-identical.
- `parkour.pdf` and `robot_parkour learning.pdf` are **different papers** despite similar names: Extreme Parkour (Cheng et al., ICRA 2024) and Robot Parkour Learning (Zhuang et al., CoRL 2023), concurrent works that cite each other.

**Filename traps.** `Spiker_A_Framework...pdf` is **Spiker+**, not the earlier Spiker paper; the filename drops the plus. `energy-efficient-implementation.pdf` gives no hint of its subject and is Harmeling et al. on spiking recurrent cells. `parkour.pdf` extracts as scrambled text because of a subset font and had to be read as rendered pages; its identifier is not printed in the file.

**How each paper obtains its energy number** - the distinction this thesis exists to make.

| provenance | papers |
|---|---|
| **Measured on real rails or a meter** | Lindqvist (board and fabric rails, 9-21 nJ per synaptic event), the neuromorphic drone (idle-versus-running delta on Loihi, 27 uJ per inference), LENS (2.7 mW with baseline subtracted), Al Hafiz (onboard current monitor, board and execution power separated), Guerra-Hernandez (bench current, 828 mW) |
| **Tool estimate** | Cerebron, Cheng 2025, FireFly-S, FireFly-P, the DATE hybrid, Spiker+, Harmeling, Li 2021, SCNN |
| **Theoretical operation counts** | **ES-Parkour**, RT-SNN, the end-to-end edge robotics platform - all multiplying operation counts by Horowitz's 45 nm constants |

**Three findings that bear directly on the thesis.**

1. **ES-Parkour's 88.3 % energy saving, the number motivating this whole project, was never measured or synthesised.** It comes from multiplying operation counts by published per-operation constants. The paper states it was never run on a robot or on neuromorphic hardware. That is precisely the claim this thesis tests.
2. **Harmeling et al. (2026) list this thesis's question as their future work**: "adaptive event-driven scheduling, and a hybrid design switching between synchronous and event-driven execution based on observed spike density". They also state that event-driven execution "could become advantageous when activity is sufficiently sparse" without determining where.
3. **Two papers argue against the event-driven premise on tool estimates.** Spiker+ finds the most power-efficient accelerators in its survey are clock-driven, and reports its own power *rising* as activity falls. The neuron-level comparative study (Marostica et al., outside this folder) finds the same and attributes it to control-circuit overhead. Both conclusions rest on Vivado, which is the strongest available argument for measuring.

**Scope.** Four papers carry `other`: two control-theory papers whose "event-triggered" and "self-triggered" refer to when to transmit a control signal rather than to spiking hardware, and two non-spiking accelerator papers (SCNN, Eyeriss) that nonetheless supply this thesis's crossover framing and its dataflow vocabulary respectively.
